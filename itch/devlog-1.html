Post title: Eighteen combinations: what the file says about the Rechlin story. Type: Game Design (or Update). Image: itch\screenshot-4-file.png (the memo of 7 February). Open this file in the browser and copy from the first paragraph down.

Most accounts of Operation Eisenhammer end the same way. In the spring of 1945, the story goes, the Luftwaffe had about a hundred Mistel ready to strike the power stations round Moscow, when a US raid on the test centre at Rechlin destroyed 18 of them, and the operation was given up.

The Luftwaffe's own file tells it differently. On microfilm T-971 in the US National Archives, roll 22, are two memos about "Unternehmen Eisenhammer". The first, of 18 January 1945, is signed by Koller, the Chief of the Luftwaffe General Staff, and ends with one line: carry out the attack. The second, of 7 February, is a briefing paper of the operations staff for Göring's meeting with Speer. It counts the targets, thirteen power stations; the Mistel needed, about a hundred; and those that exist: "Bisher sind 18 Gespanne an die Truppe ausgeliefert", so far 18 combinations have been delivered. It then explains why the attack can no longer be flown as planned: the airfields round Königsberg are lost, from west of the Oder only the plants round Tula are within reach, the fuel would be two thirds of what the air fleet defending the Reich receives in February, and fog and navigation may defeat the whole operation. Its proposal: postpone.

So the 18 were delivered, not destroyed, and the decision to shelve the operation was proposed two months before the raid on Rechlin. A study for the US Air Force of 1956 does say that Mistel meant for these attacks were lost in raids on airfields: the story has a true core. But no source found gives the number, and none makes the raid the reason.

In the study you can read both memos in facsimile, transcription and translation, and then take the staff's seat yourself: choose the targets, the airfields, the fuel and the method, and on 7 February decide whether to follow the staff or overrule it. A model runs your plan with the file's own figures, the Soviet record and assumptions it names. Most plans that attack end with a few hundred megawatts destroyed, and no effect reaching the front before the war in Europe ends on 8 May.
